`Introduction`

Luke spends a lot of time in Acts recording the unfolding of the Old Testament to the Jews through Paul, Stephen, James, and others, revealing that the Old Testament was pointing us to God's redemptive plan through Jesus, preparing us for His sacrifice all along (see Acts 7, Acts 13:13-52, Acts 15:14-21, etc). Unfolding the Old Testament is a common practice not just in Acts but much of the New Testament. Augustine says it much better than I do: "The New Testament lies hidden in the Old, and the Old Testament is unveiled in the New."

As I spend a lot of time in the Old Testament these days, I see this pattern of the New Testament being hidden in the Old everywhere I look. Jesus has been the plan all along. That brings me to a passage in my reading today, Exodus 17:8-16: __Israel Defeats Amalek__ is the subheading title for this section in the ESV.
 
Ever since God first revealed His plan to save mankind through Jesus in Genesis 3:15, the Old Testament paints a propethic picture of Jesus' redemptive work on the cross over and over again.

`Painting a Picture`

__Quick Detour__

Before I dive in, a little context for this true story in Exodus 17:8-16:

God has freed the Israelites from their bondage of Egypt, and so now they are in the wilderness and God is directing them to the Promised Land. Whilst sojourning in the wilderness they grumble because they are hungry, thirsty, etc. God hears their grumbling and takes care of them. Then they grumble again, God takes care of them again, etc. God has sent them quail, manna, water from the rock at Rephidim, and now they are camped out at Rephidim.

Amalek (grandson of Esau) and his people go to Rephidim to meet Moses and the Israelites and do battle with them. I wonder what started this dispute. Israel is sojourning in a land that doesn't belong to them presently; it could be as simple as they are encroaching on else's territory and that caused a rift, but I can only speculate with my current knowledge.

This is Moses' strategy to defeat Amalek. He tells Joshua to choose men and go fight the Amalekites. Meanwhile Moses climbs up a hill, taking Aaron his brother and Hur his friend up with him. They are overlooking the battle below and Moses holds up his wooden staff over his head. When Moses kept the staff above his head, Israel prevailed. When his arms grew tired and lowered the staff below his head, Amalek prevailed. So then Moses sat upon a stone, and with Aaron on one side and Hur on the other, they hoisted the wooden staff in Moses' arms above his head. And so Israel defeated Amalek.

__End Detour__

Have you ever seen those children's books where the pages are these transparent overlays? And when the pages are all stacked on top of each other they form one composite, cohesive image?But individually the pages only show parts or glimpses of the whole picture? I believe this is what the Old Testament does when you overlay it with the New Testament. Hear me out:

First, consider these two verses for overlay:

Mark 15:27: And with [Jesus] they crucified two robbers [on that <u>hill</u> called Golgatha], *one on his right and one on his left*. |

and

Exodus 18:10,12b ESV: Moses, Aaron, and Hur went up to the top of the <u>hill</u>...Aaron and Hur held up his hands [and the staff], *one on one side, and the other on the other side*. |

With these two verses as primer, read below and picture these two stories side by side. Do you see any overlap?

the staff on the other side. Does this picture ring any bells?

The staff in Picture 1 was the same staff that Moses used to strike the rock at Rephidim for water. The same staff of wrath that brought judgement on Egypt. One day God's staff of wrath would be used to strike onto His own son, like when Moses struck the rock. On the cross, it pleased God to strike His Son the Cornerstone with His staff, the cross.


`Conclusion`

I believe this image of Moses on the hill is one of the earliest times we see a very specific, clear prophecy of the cross in the Old Testament, on that hill at Rephidim. But unlike Moses, who needed the two's help to lift up the staff, not only was Jesus able see it through to the very end to lift the staff of God's wrath up willingly, but he offers to lift up the staff of God's wrath from not only the two thieves on his left and right but for all the world.

"And Joshua overwhelmed Amalek and his people with the sword.. and Moses built an altar and called the name of it, The LORD Is My Banner" (Ex 17:15-16 ESV). That is how the battle against Amalek ended for the Israelites because of Moses and that is how the battle with Satan will end for those of us in Jesus because of Jesus.

__Fun fact:__ Rephidim, where this story of Moses on the hill took place, means support or structure. Nothing with God is an accident.
