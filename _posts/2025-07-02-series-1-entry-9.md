The first few words of Tenth Avenue North's song _Worn_ bounce around in my head a lot these days:

{% highlight text %}
I'm tired
I'm worn
My heart is heavy
{% endhighlight %}

In the midst of big reroutings of future plans, I find myself struggling to get through the day. I need God so much, but it is hard to even find the words to ask for help. I am realizing that I am at a point in my journey where I am met by two demons standing in my path: Apathy and his close relative, Sloth. I find myself stuck here against these formidable foes, unable to continue forward on my journey to the Celestial City. I am here writing as I am reminded of something Kirby Kelly talked about on the Christian Habits podcast with Barb Raveling: she says that if you are struggling against a particular sin, make a battle plan against it. So in the spirit of making a battle plan, I have decided to write a letter to these two demons, in the name of Jesus, titled _An Invitation to Surrender_.

_`An Invitation to Surrender`_

Before I personally say anything against my demonic enemies, I am going to bring into remembrance the words and actions of Joshua, who represents Jesus. This is not only an attack and a strategy against demonic forces, but also an encouragement to you, O reader, to do the same to the enemy's minions you meet on your path to heaven.

_keep in mind that Jesus is the Greek version of Joshua, which means **Yahweh is salvation** ("Yeho-shua")_

|I am here to remind you, enemy of God, of Joshua's words in Joshua 10:25: "Do not be afraid or dismayed; be strong and courageous. For thus the LORD will do to all your enemies" and then Joshua strikes the 5 enemy kings, kills them, hangs them on 5 trees, and then throws them into the cave and seals them in with rocks covering the entrance. Joshua is an Old Testament representation of Jesus. This story shows you and I what Jesus did on the cross. Jesus, having put on the serpentine flesh of humanity, subjected his own body to the same fate as the five kings. He subjected himself to the terrible fate his enemies deserved. He allowed himself to be struck (by the high priests and the Romans), hung on a tree, he died, and then was buried in a cave. Unlike the 5 Amorite kings in Joshua 10, however, Jesus did not remain in the cave. He arose! He defeated death. He came out of the cave when God rose Him from the dead. He left the evil kings of sin and death behind in that cave, buried; they did not come out with him. For the first time ever, a human did not succumb to the temptations of the flesh nor did He succumb to the ultimate destiny of the flesh: death. Jesus won! And now, like the Old Testament Joshua defeated the evil Amorite kings for His people, Israel, the New Testament Jesus provides victory over the king of evil himself, satan, for all of us, both the Greek (Gentile) and Jew alike! For those of us who have submitted to the kingship of Christ, dying to ourselves and putting on Christ, Jesus proclaims to us like Joshua did, "Do not be afraid or dismayed; be strong and courageous. **For thus the LORD will do to all your enemies against whom you fight.**" Jesus has already defeated the devil, but it has not yet been totally finalized. _`Jesus has already defeated you, demons of Sloth and Apathy that stand in my path; This is your invitation to surrender. I put my feet on your neck by the power of my King Jesus.`_ So I have confidence in the victory of Jesus and in that confidence I pick up the Sword of the Spirit and push onward! There is more conquest to be had, more Jerichos to be toppled! More Rahabs to be rescued! More plunder to be dedicated to the LORD! ONWAAAARD!|

`So what exactly is my plan? To ask Jesus to help me act like the battle is already won and then by His victory to live through the truth that the battle is already won. This is not a natural way of thinking, fighting like the fight is already won, but it is how Jesus commands us to think. Mold me! Shape me! Whatever it takes to be an effective vessel for You, O LORD!`
