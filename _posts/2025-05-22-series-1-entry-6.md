\**bandaid ripping off sound*\*: I struggle with sloth. I want to be a high achiever for Christ and it is so hard to be a failure and miss the mark so often.

I have been reminded recently, from the gospel of John, from listening to [this podcast episode](https://barbraveling.com/kirby-kelly-you-can-be-free-overcoming-temptation-and-habitual-sin-by-the-power-and-promises-of-the-gospel/), and in many other ways (God has just been sprinkling this reminder everywhere), that *I need the power of the gospel just as much now as I did when I first turned to Jesus*. Sometimes I get real focused on wanting to help others and I neglect that I need to be bathing myself in the gospel too. I want to be a healthy tree in God's garden so that I can produce lots of fruits/seeds. So, how can I be a high producing fruit tree for Christ? How much of it is my own efforts? How much of it is God's efforts? This is the question I wrestle with these days. I believe water-walking Jesus catching up with the boat-rowing disciples in John 6:16-21 gives insight into that question. Here's the passage:

{% highlight text %}
When evening came, his disciples
went down to the sea, got into a
boat, and started across the sea
to Capernaum. It was now dark,
and Jesus had not yet come to them.
The sea became rough because a
strong wind was blowing. When they
had rowed about three or four
miles, they saw Jesus walking on
the sea and coming near the boat,
and they were frightened. But he
said to them, “It is I; do not
be afraid.” Then they were glad
to take him into the boat, and
immediately the boat was at the
land to which they were going.
{% endhighlight %}

In my struggle with sloth, and my asking King Jesus ("Yahweh saves") to rescue me, He has made this passage stand out in my mind and so I want to write and meditate on it and in the process help you see what He has helped me glean from it. A couple things I want to point out about this story:

1. Jesus let them row for "three or four miles" before He showed up
2. The end of this story is super interesting: when they took Jesus into the boat, it seems to me that they were *teleported* to their destination. Carefully read the last sentence (v. 21)

With these two points in mind, I want to reflect on an important takeaway for me as I consider this story of Jesus and the disciples crossing the Sea of Galilee. Jesus disappeared earlier up to the mountain because the well-fed five thousand were going to make him king, and so his disciples got into the boat and headed across to Capernaum without Him. What is interesting is that Jesus allowed His disciples to row three or four miles in rough seas and strong winds and it isn't until after this He shows up, gets in the boat and teleports them to their destination.

This story speaks to the relationship between our own free will and God's sovereignty. This story answers the question that I often struggle with, especially when lacking motivation: "Do I need to pull myself up by my bootstraps here and accomplish my own tasks or will God accomplish them for me?" This story shows us that the answer is clearly a both/and situation. I need to suck it up and work hard at my God-given tasks even when I don't feel like it; however, that does not mean that it is up to me at the end of day to ensure they all get done. The onus is on God, and this story and many others emphasize this idea. Here me out:

Example 1: __The disciples start the journey across the Sea of Galilee alone, but then Jesus shows up, finishes the job and gets them across.__ The disciples set out on their own, fighting wind and waves for 3-4 miles, and Jesus is nowhere in sight. They have been putting in the work, they've been making good progress. That is when Jesus shows up and literally teleports them the rest of the way to the shores of Capernaum (John 6:16-21)

Example 2: __Noah built the ark first, then God closed the door.__ Think about it; God gave Noah these instructions on how to build the ark and then leaves it to Noah to manage the project and make it happen (Obviously God was ultimately there all along and helped Noah along the way, and important thing to keep in mind with all these examples). Nevertheless, the ark is designed (by God) in a way that makes it impossible for man to close the door to the ark to protect everyone and everything without from the aquatic apocalypse without. God finishes the job (Genesis 6:14, 7:16)

Example 3: __Joshua's priests stepped into the raging waters of the Jordan first, and then God parted the water second.__ Notice the sequence of events here. God did not part the waters first, they had to step out in faith into the raging waters first (Joshua 3:14-17).

So, to bring it back to the original question: __How can I be a high producing fruit tree for Christ? How much of it is my own efforts? How much of it is God's efforts?__ Well, the simple answer is that it is all Christ, but I think that answer needs to be defined/clarified further. Jesus could just do it all Himself, but He wants to share in the adventure with us. So He puts us in situations where we are forced to work hard/suffer (like the disciples rowing in a storm for 3-4 miles) because "suffering produce endurance, endurance produces character, character produces hope..." (Romans 5:3). This grows us and strengthens us to become more like him, increasing our hero-like qualities, enabling us to be able to truly contribute to the adventure more and more.

To be Christ-like, we must be willing to humble ourselves and make the first move when struggling with lack of motivation. Struggling to get started on a God-given task/project/major life change but it feels like Jesus is nowhere in sight? You and I need to just get in the boat and start rowing. Blood, sweat and tears baby. Remember, Jesus the Son of God who can walk on the water that we can hardly row ourselves through, He'll show up and finish the job. Guaranteed.

A final word. Remember that whenever we desire to do good, evil is close by (Romans 7:21) Be watchful. Yahweh give us Your elevated perspective to see the enemy's plans from miles away.

Isaiah 12:2 says: "Look here! God is my salvation. I will trust and will not be afraid. For my strength and my song is the LORD GOD. He has become my salvation."
