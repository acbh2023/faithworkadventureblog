`Intro`
I’ve been reading through my late father’s blog recently. A family member preserved it into a physical copy format before the website was taken down, which is so awesome. It is really important to be able to see my father’s heart for the kingdom of King Jesus.

My father wrote in the form of a daily devotional, and one of the the verses I read today from his devotional was Matthew 16:24:

{% highlight text %}
“If anyone would come after me,
let him deny himself and take
up his cross and follow me."
{% endhighlight %}

__What does this verse mean? How can I practically apply this?__

`DETOUR`
My previous blog on May 22 was me being transparent about my struggle with laziness; I am encouraged to say that since that blog God has helped me in those moments of laziness to overcome. Not perfectly, but improved. I want to continue to write about my efforts to overcome the laziness of my flesh. As a new creation in Christ, it is important that we don’t say “I am lazy”, but “I struggle with laziness”, or whatever the struggle may be. Paul speaks to this in his discussion on the battle between his flesh and saved soul in Romans 7. “For I do not do what I want, but I do the very thing I hate…So now it is no longer I who do it, but sin that dwells within me.” Romans (7:15b, 17). I have heard these verses about the tension between our saved soul and our flesh coexisting explained this way: imagine you have an irremovable thorn stuck in your finger. You would not say that thorn is a part of who you are, but it is in you. As renewed creations, our sinful nature, for a time, is in us but not of us.

`BODY`
Back to the question: How do I pick up my cross and follow Jesus? What is my cross? I believe the cross represents our flesh. A cross, like our dying, broken flesh, is a dead/dying organism. Our flesh is our cross, a dead flesh tree. Psalms 1 talks about the blessed man is “like a tree planted by streams of water that yields its fruit in season and whose leaf does not wither”. The Blessed Man, Jesus, has paved that path for us of how to go from being a dead tree to a thriving, fruit-bearing tree and was the first one to complete the entire process (death, burial, resurrection). He is now in His perfected, resurrected body. How did he get there? By carrying his cross upon His back and dying to Himself and His fleshly desires for the sake of sinners. That is our job, too. We know, like us, even he struggled with not wanting to carry his cross: `“My Father, if it is possible, let this cup [of wrath] pass from Me; nevertheless, not as I will, but as You will.”` (Matthew 26:39b).

So, like Jesus carrying a dead tree on his back, I must fight against my lazy flesh and its desire to hold me back, and discipline it, even if that means throwing it upon my back (with Jesus' help) kicking and screaming and marching onward towards the prize of heaven. And it is important to remember, even Jesus Himself had help carrying his cross up to the hill called Golgatha. We must bear each other’s dead flesh trees together. What does that look like? Sometimes it means cheering each other on, but other times we must exhort and warn each other to not give up, which does not always as fun as cheering. Hebrews 3:13 says `“But exhort one another every day, as long as it is called “today,” that none of you may be hardened by the deceitfulness of sin.”` The Greek for the word exhort here is *parakaleo* which literally means to “call to the side”. It is this idea of pulling someone aside and urging them to pursue a certain course of conduct. The world often does not affirm this form of friendly help; warning your neighbor of the danger of their sinful conduct is not a popular behavior these days (to be clear, the way we warn a self-professed Christian and a self-professed lost soul should look different). But if we really believe that some really serious stuff is at stake, heaven and hell, life and death, we must act with frantic urgency like Mary did when she discovered Jesus was alive at the tomb and ran to tell the disciples (Matthew 28:8).

One day, Jesus will cast off the dead tree we carry upon our backs, and we will be transformed completely and finally into a tree whose leaves never wither. To be clear, that is who we are now in Christ, a tree planted by streams of water that yields its fruit in season, but as it says in 1 John 3:2, `“What we will be has not yet appeared; but we know that when [Jesus] appears we shall be like Him, because we shall see Him as He is.”` We are not in our final forms yet.

`Conclusion`
Our dead flesh tree is the means through which God brings salvation to the world, Jesus working with us and through us. This bag of bones we carry around that is bound by the law of sin, is the means through which God brings salvation to the world. So, you and I must pick up your dead flesh tree and march onward to the Celestial City, proclaiming the hope of King Jesus Christ, the tree of life, to the world along the way.

Verse to meditate on |Philippians 1:21: `“To live is Christ, and to die is gain.”` Keep persevering! Keep yourselves in the love of God. Share the hope and light of Jesus to those you run into daily, be intentional, be shrewd. A lot is at stake.|
