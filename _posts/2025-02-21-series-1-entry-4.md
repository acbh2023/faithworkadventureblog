`Intro` There are two parts to our relationship with God, 1) faith and 2) works, faith being our private, personal time with God and works being our public time with God. In the book of James we learn that faith and works are inseparable. Really we should not ever use these two words apart. I propose a new word to be used when we discuss our relationship with God; it shouldn't be spoken of as simply "my faith" but should be "my working faith", "my active faith", or "my living faith" as a reminder of the oneness of faith and works. Another way to look at it is Jesus' summation of the Old Testament. The two most important commands are 1) to Love the Lord God with all your will, your actions, and your resources and 2) to love your neighbor as yourself; there's that private and public dimension to our relationship with God again. As lovers of Jesus, we love our quiet, alone time with Him, but that's not enough to have a good relationship with Him; we must go out and care for people physically and ultimately spiritually. In the same way that Jonah didn't stay in the whale and Jesus didn't stay in the grave, we too cannot stay in the comfort of the womb of our new birth.

__DETOUR__ Before I proceed, I am writing this sitting from the comfort of my couch, sipping on my latte, safe inside from the freezing cold outside, and that's not the case for everyone here in Kansas City today. God please help those who are cold and hungry and help me and the readers to be stirred to help them. Before we get into it, I want to be clear that these admonitions for you to live a life of action is not just for you, but for me also. I like to think of these blogs just as much self-directed as they are directed at you, reader. Like Jonah, I too struggle with being a bitter-hearted hypocrite.

`Main Body: Our alone time with God is not enough`


__First Point:__ Jonah didn't stay in the whale.

Jonah didn't stay in the whale, but based on his annoyed reaction to Nineveh's repentance, part of me wonders if he would have stayed in there if he had the choice. Jonah didn't ask to be spat out, God commanded the fish to spit him out. To me Jonah is a warning about being a 'Hermit Christian'; you'll see more what I mean in a bit. I'm not bashing private time with God, just the opposite. It's so important and beautiful and Jonah captures the beauty of it well. For Jonah, although a difficult memory, probably one of his sweetest, most intimate times with God and most powerful memories in his life was his time in the whale. Read these excerpts from this beautiful prayer from Jonah in Chapter 2 Verses 1-10 where he recalls the moments before the whale saved him as he was drowning and following. This prayer reminds me of the sweet intimacy of some of my personal, private, intimate times with the Lord (feel free to read all verses, Jonah 2:1-10, in your Bible if you'd like):

{% highlight text %}
"I called out to Yahweh
and He answered me
...
For You cast me into the deep,
all Your waves and billows
passed over me
...
I went down [and] the earth's bars
closed upon me forever
...
Yet You brought up my life from
the pit, O Yahweh my God
...
When my life as fainting away, I
remembered Yahweh, and my prayer
went up to You, into
Your holy temple"
{% endhighlight %}

His prayer is so beautiful and so relatable, his perspective, his desperation, just wow. Let's break down a couple things in that prayer:

1) __*You* cast me into the deep / *Your* waves and billows:__ Isn't it amazing the perspective God has given Jonah about his life-destroying circumstances? He recognizes that the waves and massive billows that were drowning him are *God's* waves and billows. He recognizes that it was in fact *God* himself that threw him into the deep. Not only that but Jonah praises God because he recognizes God's good purposes for putting him through terrible circumstances. Although this is difficult, next time you go through something terrible, or when you reflect on past trauma, remember like Jonah that it was God that willed it and was in control of that difficult situation. He wounds and He heals. He is able to stop horrible things from happening, but sometimes He doesn't. Can you be objective about your trauma like Job after he realizes what a mistake it was to question God about his life circumstances and say, "Sorry God that I questioned You about things I don't actually understand" (Job 42:3)? Can you be objective about the terrible things that happened to you and recognize that God has a bigger plan and it's not all about you? Jonah was a total screw-up and he was able to. So are we and so can we, God help us. | 2) __My prayer went (up) to you, into Your holy temple:__ A couple things here that I think is cool. How is Jonah's prayer reaching God's holy temple? Jonah historically is placed during the reign of Jeroboam II of the Northern Kingdom of Isreal (2 Kings 14:23-25), an idolatrous king who did evil like Jeraboam I before him, and so they didn't have a Yahweh temple (Bethel and Dan had idolatrous golden calves for worship, but not a Yahweh temple). The southern kingdom of Judah had Solomon's temple, but Jonah was not from the Southern Kingdom. So the point is Jonah didn't have a actual physical temple to pray to, and at the bottom of the Mediterranean Sea he was nowhere near his homeland anyways, so how did his prayer get into Yahweh's __holy temple__ and why are the prayers going *up* to get there? As the story has progressed, we have been hearing about Jonah being on a journey of downward self-destruction. Jonah 1:3 he "went down to Joppa" and "went down" to where the ship was, and then even once he's on the ship the captain discovers he had "gone down" below deck. And then in 2:1-10 we hear an account of him going further down as he sinks down into the depths of the ocean. Jonah is going down, *down*, __*down*__, even to the "realm of the dead" (2:2). Have you ever heard the phrase, "When you hit rock bottom, the only way to look is up"? This is the implication of the point Jonah reached in 2:6: Rock bottom. Jonah had many chances along the way to stop and climb out of this pit. When Jonah began to flee God's command he could have turned back as he was going down to Joppa, down to the docks, etc.; he had many opportunities to turn back, but he didn't. This is me. I am Jonah. He doesn't stop until his only option is to cry out for help because it's too late for him to stop himself as he sinks down to death. He has built up too much momentum now, rolling down the mountain and at the base of the mountain are the jaws of hell, wide open and he's headed right for them. That was me. So he's at the bottom of the bottom, the only way to look is up. And he does. He cries upward for help and God hears him. But there is this huge chasm between them, Jonah is down in the deep, in the realm of the dead, and God is on high, seemingly nowhere in sight, and it begs the question, "Who shall ascend the mountain of Yahweh?" (Ps 24:3). Who will cross the chasm? Who will close the gap? Jonah knows there's only one fit for the job, and it ain't himself. Jonah's hope is in God's Holy Temple, Jesus Christ, when like the fish ascending from the deep rescued Jonah from the darkness, the Living Temple Himself came down to the depths to rescue us and when He ascended back up the mountain called Golgatha, brought us up from the depths with Him, where we were crucified with Him (Gal 2:20). When Jonah knew he was driven away from God's sight, he looked again toward Your Holy Temple, Jesus Christ. As it says in 1 Peter 1:12, Jonah had Christ and His good news in mind when He wrote this book. What a beautiful prayer|

So, we've established private, intimate time with the the LORD is beautiful, but it is not a complete picture of an active faith. Jonah couldn't stay in the whale, and we can't stay in the womb of our new birth. When it comes to being a born again follower of Jesus, I have a tendency to, in the words of Nicodemus, try to crawl back inside the womb, so to speak (John 3). It is warm, cozy, and safe in there. I have a reluctance to climb out of the cozy womb of my morning quiet time and go out into the world and live out my faith. That was Jonah's problem too. Even after being rescued by God through the whale and being spit out, given a second chance to do his job, Jonah still has the same heart problem, he hates that God forgives Nineveh. He's worshipful in the privateness whale but hateful and angry in his heart when it comes to the public aspect of his faith. God help us. He throws a tantrum because God relents. Jonah is worshipful and totally in love with God while He is in the safety of the womb of the whale, but when He is spat out of the whale, born again with a second life and a second chance to obey, he doesn't care for the salvation that God brought about to Nineveh through him. Is there a certain group of people you hate and have written off as undeserving of the good news of God's mercy? Be careful. When God rescued me from the dark depths of my sin, he brought me hope and light and a sweet time of connection and growth as he rescued me from the depths via His Fish of Salvation, Jesus. I must not stay in those dark depths where God brought me light, but I must share that light which God showed me in the darkness to my fellow Ninevites, __<u>especially</u>__ the ones I think are undeserving. *Fellow* Ninevites, you say? Indeed. Jonah thought that, unlike himself, the Ninevites deserved every ounce of God's wrath and more. God helped reveal to Jonah that he was actually just like them, but worse, Jonah was a Ninevite in his heart. So, like Jonah, I too am a Ninevite at heart. A former child of the devil; aren't we all? So we must not go to our Ninevehs (our mission fields) with a 'holier than thou' mentality. We must not think that we are personally saving the world, with an us vs. them mentality. Our human enemies are not our real enemies, our real struggle is against spiritual forces of darkness. Instead of a savior mentality it should be rather the opposite, we must enter Nineveh seeing ourselves in the Ninevites around us, in desperate need of a Savior. That is why sanctification is considered a lifelong process and not an instant process. I am in desperate need of a Savior now just as much and more than when I first decided to follow Jesus at 4 years old.

__Second Point:__ Jesus didn't stay in the grave.

If we claim to follow Jesus but do not walk out of our tombs like He did to spread The Good News and help the afflicted, but remain in our tomb, we are an embarrasment to our Father and are not actually following in our brother Jesus' footsteps. On the day of His resurrection He left the tomb and began a new work bringing about new creation, preparing the way for the new heavens and the new earth as John prepared the way for Jesus; we must do the same, or when the time comes, He will say "Depart from me, I never knew you". He will go from being our Loving Father to our worst enemy real quick. May we all be warned, deep in our hearts, so that the fear of God is in us, so that we do not sin. To not live a life of sacrificing our time for His work (caring for people physically and ultimately spiritually) would be a grave sin. We would be like the guest at the king's wedding that had no wedding garment. Jesus is the wedding garment that we are to "put on" as it says in Galatians 3:27. If we are wearing Jesus, unlike normal wedding garments, the Kingdom Wedding Garments will take control of your body and animate you like a puppet, going where the Grand Puppeteer motions and acting as the Grand Puppeteer leads. How sad and pointless would it be for God to raise us from the dead only for us to stay in our sepulchre. May it never be for you or me.

I see that there are two main aspects to the works part of active faith, 1) sharing the good news of the resurrected Jesus Christ that we can follow and through Him escape death and also 2) as James says "visiting orphans and widows in their affliction". God has been convicting me recently to be better about helping the needy and afflicted; we cannot neglect that side of an active faith. I want to share that with you because if you are not actively helping the needy and afflicted in some way or are not actively struggling with the fact that you're not but you should be, you better beg God to give you a tender heart towards the needy and afflicted. He has been warning me about this recently. We need to be at the forefront of this. I like to say that every believer is called to be a herald of good news of Jesus Christ's death and resurrection, but only some are called to it full-time. The rest are called part-time. There is no way around that. I am beginning to think the same of helping the poor and afflicted. Not all of us are called to full-time charity work, helping the homeless etc., but we are all called to it part time, and that includes when it is inconvenient to our plans

__Conclusion__

When we look at Jonah's story, I see my story; an eventually obedient servant of God's Great Commission that is still battling the diseased, bitter heart within; even after our new birth, the battle with the flesh continues. Our flesh is no longer of us, but is in us, like a splinter in your finger. One day God will pull out His tweezers of vengeance, and fully and completely remove the splinter, give us a new, sprinter-proof, disease-proof, pain proof finger and a whole new body while He's at it, a body that runs like the wind and does not grow weary, a body that does "kneed" for knee replacements, a body that is cancer-proof, a body that does not experience brain fog, depression, ADHD, and on and on. But in the mean time, when we see Jonah being spat out onto his mission field, we should see a picture of our new birth and what should follow. What is our job when we've left the womb of salvation? To go out to our mission field and proclaim good news. When we see Jonah after his "new birth" being bitter about God saving the Ninevites, we should be reminded that a follower of Jesus, even after being born again will still struggle with sin and must ask God to keep our hearts in check. When see Jonah's bitter heart after doing his mission we should see our own bitter, rotting hearts. After Nineveh repents, Jonah goes off and pouts. As he sits there stewing, asking God to destroy Nineveh, God grows a shady plant for him to protect him from the heat while he pouts, but then God quickly kills the plant and Jonah is hot again. He throws a tantrum and is distraught. He'd rather protect himself from a little heat from the sun than protect an entire city from the heat of the fire of God's destruction. He doesn't care about people's souls he just wants comfort. God puts on this little drama for us with the plant to expose Jonah's rotten heart. Does that sound familiar? Do minor life inconveniences expose the rotten state of your heart? They expose mine for sure. Remember those minor life inconveniences come from God. Even satan's attack on Job's life was ultimately God's orchestration. God help us to zoom way out from the minor inconveniences of our life and see the big picture, see the suffering around us. Does it break your heart that someone in your community might freeze to death tonight? Does your heart break that fathers, mothers, and children in Sudan are dying like dogs in the street every day? Does it bother you that so many people are rejecting God and spending an eternity in hell? Sometimes I struggle with feeling compassion. God give us compassion.

Like the story of Nicodemus, the story of Jonah ends with what I like to think of as a choose your own ending scenario. It is unclear whether Nicodemus followed Jesus as it is unclear if Jonah repented of his stubborn bitterness and returned to serving the Lord. I believe that is intentional. What we do know, looking at Jonah's prayer in 2:1-10 is that Jonah was someone that Jonah was someone familiar with the Scriptures, so we can assume he had an intimate relationship with God's Word. But that obviously didn't matter because we learn that he doesn't like being a doer of the word. Part of building an intimate relationship with the Lord is putting on the shoes of the gospel of peace and letting them take you where they lead you. Whether they take you to the homeless man under the bridge, to the person next to you at the grocery store, or halfway across the earth in the jungles of Kalimantan, Indonesia, don't ignore His direction. Listen to Him; ask Him to help you listen. And remember, there's still hope for our rotten hearts! God used Jonah to turn Nineveh toward Himself despite Jonah's bad attitude. He still uses us even on our bad days. We cannot think we can only serve God on days when we have a good attitude. And the other final encouragement is that while we cannot crawl back into the womb of our salvation, we have a God that is with us around wherever we go, hiding us in the shadow of His wings, acting as our mother hen, our fortress, our shield, and our bulwark. Unlike when a baby is born in the physical world, as the spiritually reborn, we are able to always be in the womb of the comfort of our Father, like the tabernacle tent in the desert, moving with the Israelites wherever they go, enveloping us in His nourishing comfort and safety. A mobile womb, a mobile fortress, a mobile tent of safety. And so now I leave it to you, reader. Choose your own ending. Will you quietly and obediently allow the Potter shape you, the clay, as He pleases, sending you where He pleases and doing with you as He pleases?
